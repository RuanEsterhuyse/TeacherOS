# Instruction Designer

## Purpose

The Instruction Designer converts curriculum analysis into an effective, classroom-ready lesson design. It determines how the lesson should unfold, how students will engage with the content, where modeling, guided practice, collaboration, and independent work belong, and where additional scaffolds or checks for understanding are justified.

The module makes the analyzed curriculum practical and teachable without changing its required goals. It preserves required content, records all TeacherOS enhancements, and prepares a complete instructional plan for the Slide Designer.

## Responsibilities

The Instruction Designer is responsible for:

- preserving required objectives, texts, activities, and assessments;
- organizing the lesson into a coherent instructional sequence;
- clarifying the purpose of every lesson phase;
- designing appropriate openings, transitions, modeling, guided practice, collaboration, independent practice, reflection, and closure;
- adding purposeful checks for understanding;
- planning meaningful student participation;
- designing discussion structures;
- adding vocabulary instruction where needed;
- adding appropriate ELD and accessibility supports;
- addressing supported misconceptions and barriers;
- maintaining the curriculum’s intended academic rigor;
- adapting pacing when evidence and classroom constraints justify it;
- preparing instructional content for the Slide Designer;
- documenting all TeacherOS additions and adaptations;
- preserving curriculum traceability for every major decision;
- maintaining visible unresolved issues for teacher review.

The module must separate source requirements from instructional interpretation and enhancement. It may improve delivery, clarity, access, and participation, but it may not obscure what the curriculum originally required.

## Non-Responsibilities

The Instruction Designer must not:

- remove required curriculum content without explicit teacher authorization;
- change the central learning goal;
- replace a required assessment without clearly labeling and authorizing the change;
- invent claims about the curriculum or source materials;
- design final slide layouts or visual composition;
- generate final images;
- render Google Slides or another presentation format;
- send content directly to a presentation renderer;
- hide TeacherOS adaptations inside curriculum-original content;
- reduce rigor merely to make the lesson simpler;
- resolve critical source conflicts without an authorized decision.

The module creates the instructional design that downstream presentation modules represent. It does not perform final presentation design or rendering.

## Inputs

The Instruction Designer requires:

- **Enriched Structured Lesson Model:** The source-grounded model with curriculum-analysis sections populated.
- **Central learning goal:** The primary outcome and expected evidence of mastery.
- **Standards-objective map:** Explicit and inferred relationships among standards and objectives.
- **Knowledge and skill map:** Required concepts, procedures, skills, language, and prerequisites.
- **Activity-purpose map:** The analyzed purpose, demand, dependencies, and evidence for every source activity.
- **Essentiality classification:** Required, supporting, optional, or unclear status with evidence.
- **Assessment-alignment map:** Connections among objectives, assessments, demand, and expected evidence.
- **Vocabulary analysis:** Essential terms, source instruction, language demands, and support opportunities.
- **Text and reading analysis:** Reading purposes, modes, demands, evidence expectations, and barriers.
- **Pacing analysis:** Source timing, load, conflicts, and multi-day implications.
- **Barrier analysis:** Source-explicit, strongly supported, and tentative access concerns.
- **Misconception analysis:** Supported likely errors and monitoring points.
- **Existing-supports inventory:** Modeling, scaffolds, ELD supports, accommodations, and other source-provided support.
- **Instructional-coherence report:** Strong, weak, missing, and conflicting lesson relationships.
- **Fidelity and traceability map:** Source evidence and analytical interpretations for major curriculum elements.
- **Unresolved-issues list:** Open conflicts, missing evidence, and decisions requiring review.
- **TeacherOS Instructional Design Principles:** The governing philosophy and decision hierarchy.
- **Teacher preferences, when available:** Authorized preferences concerning routines, pacing, grouping, format, or classroom delivery.

Teacher preferences may shape delivery but cannot silently override required curriculum or the central learning goal.

## Input Validation

Before lesson design begins, the Instruction Designer verifies that:

- the central learning goal and evidence of mastery are identifiable;
- required activities and assessments are known;
- unresolved critical source issues are visible;
- the activity sequence is reliable;
- required texts and materials are available or explicitly flagged;
- essentiality classifications have evidence;
- the curriculum analysis is complete enough for safe design;
- teacher-requested changes are distinguishable from source requirements.

Incomplete noncritical information may proceed with visible qualifications. When critical information is missing, the module must return a blocked-design report rather than guess. The report must identify the blocking condition, affected lesson components, safe partial work, and recommended human decisions.

## Instructional Design Goals

The Instruction Designer seeks to:

- protect curriculum fidelity;
- improve instructional clarity;
- increase meaningful student participation;
- improve coherence across lesson phases;
- support diverse learners;
- preserve appropriate academic rigor;
- reduce unnecessary teacher preparation and navigation work;
- produce a lesson that is immediately teachable.

These goals operate under the TeacherOS decision hierarchy. When they conflict, learning and curriculum fidelity take priority over convenience or presentation preference.

## Lesson Architecture

Lesson architecture is a flexible organization of instructional phases. The curriculum, central learning goal, student demands, and available time determine which phases are appropriate and how they relate.

### Opening

The opening gains attention, establishes a purposeful entry into the lesson, connects to relevant prior learning, and prepares students for the work ahead. It should be concise and directly related to the central goal.

### Objective and Success Criteria

This phase communicates what students will learn and how successful learning will be demonstrated. Language should be understandable to students while remaining faithful to the curriculum objective.

### Background Knowledge

This phase activates or builds only the knowledge essential for accessing the lesson. It should reveal readiness and address necessary context without introducing unrelated information or replacing required learning.

### Vocabulary Preparation

This phase prepares only the words essential for access or participation at that point in the lesson. Curriculum-provided vocabulary instruction must remain distinct from TeacherOS-added support.

### Modeling

Modeling demonstrates the target thinking, skill, process, or response features. It makes relevant expert thinking visible without overexplaining or completing all cognitive work for students.

### Guided Practice

Guided practice shifts responsibility toward students while support and feedback remain available. It includes evidence that helps the teacher decide whether students are ready for greater independence.

### Collaborative Practice

Collaborative practice uses structured peer interaction to advance the objective. Roles, directions, expected evidence, accountability, and participation routines must be clear.

### Independent Practice

Independent practice gives students an opportunity to apply or demonstrate learning without immediate peer or teacher completion of the task. The task should reflect the preparation provided earlier in the lesson.

### Assessment

Assessment preserves required curriculum measures and integrates appropriate formative evidence. Every measure must connect directly to an objective and a clear instructional decision or success criterion.

### Reflection and Closure

Reflection and closure return students to the learning goal, consolidate important understanding, make progress visible, and clarify homework or next steps when applicable.

Not every lesson requires every phase. Phases may be combined or revisited when the curriculum and learning purpose support that choice. The architecture must clarify and strengthen the source lesson rather than impose a generic sequence upon it.

## Instructional Sequence Design

Every lesson phase must define:

- **Phase name:** A stable, descriptive identifier.
- **Instructional purpose:** The phase’s contribution to learning.
- **Connection to objective:** The specific objective or success criterion advanced.
- **Duration:** Source timing, proposed timing, and any justified adjustment.
- **Teacher actions:** Observable facilitation, explanation, questioning, modeling, or monitoring.
- **Student actions:** Observable thinking, speaking, listening, reading, writing, or production.
- **Teacher language:** Concise directions, questions, explanations, or think-aloud guidance.
- **Student task:** The work or evidence students are expected to produce.
- **Grouping structure:** Whole class, group, partner, independent, or an intentional sequence of formats.
- **Materials:** Required texts, pages, tools, handouts, and technology.
- **Checks for understanding:** Evidence gathered and the instructional decision it informs.
- **Support level:** Modeling, guided support, collaborative support, independent performance, or another explicit level.
- **Transition:** The conceptual and procedural link to the next phase.
- **Source reference:** The curriculum evidence supporting required content and sequence.
- **Adaptation status:** The applicable design-origin label and rationale.

Phases must remain ordered and connected to required curriculum components. If a field does not apply, the design should mark it accordingly rather than omit it silently.

## Adaptation Status

### Curriculum Original

The content, task, sequence, wording, support, or structure comes directly from the curriculum and is preserved without substantive change.

### Curriculum Clarification

TeacherOS clarifies directions, organization, terminology, or presentation while preserving the task, demand, and intended outcome.

### TeacherOS Enhancement

TeacherOS adds justified modeling, support, interaction, explanation, transition, or formative evidence while preserving the central goal and required content.

### Teacher-Requested Change

The teacher explicitly requests or authorizes a change. The record must preserve the request, affected curriculum requirement, and instructional consequences.

### Required Deviation

A change is proposed because the lesson cannot be delivered as written, such as when materials are missing, source requirements conflict, or stated timing is impossible under confirmed constraints. A required deviation remains proposed until authorized.

Every clarification, enhancement, requested change, and deviation must include a rationale and traceability record. An adaptation label identifies origin and authority; it does not by itself establish quality or approval.

## Pacing Design

The Instruction Designer compares stated timing with realistic classroom demands while preserving required work. It considers:

- source-provided lesson and activity duration;
- time needed for directions, distribution, grouping, and transitions;
- reading, writing, discussion, and cognitive load;
- modeling and guided-practice needs;
- checks for understanding and likely response time;
- essential work compared with curriculum-labeled time-permitting work;
- implications for more than one instructional period.

The module may suggest pacing adjustments, phase boundaries, or a multi-day structure when justified. It must record the original timing, proposed timing, rationale, and effect on required content. It must never silently shorten, omit, or relabel required components to make a lesson fit.

## Teacher Modeling

Modeling should be used only when it helps students meet the objective. The design must identify exactly what the teacher demonstrates, which part of expert thinking should be visible, and how the model prepares students for guided and independent work.

Think-alouds should be concise and focused on transferable decisions or processes. Modeling must not solve the entire student task, reveal required independent answers, or remove productive cognitive demand. The model should end with a clear shift of responsibility to students.

## Student Participation

The lesson should include intentional opportunities, as appropriate, for students to:

- speak;
- listen;
- read;
- write;
- work with a partner;
- participate in group discussion;
- think independently;
- retrieve prior learning;
- apply knowledge or skills;
- select, cite, or explain evidence.

Participation must be academically meaningful and connected to the objective. The design should balance processing and performance, broaden participation beyond volunteers, and avoid activities that create movement or talk without worthwhile thinking.

## Discussion Design

Every designed discussion defines:

- **Discussion purpose:** The understanding, reasoning, language, or evidence the discussion develops.
- **Prompt:** A clear question or task aligned to the purpose.
- **Preparation time:** Individual thinking, annotation, writing, or rehearsal before participation.
- **Grouping structure:** Partner, small group, whole class, or a planned progression.
- **Sentence supports:** Optional language support matched to the discussion demand.
- **Evidence expectations:** The text, reasoning, examples, or prior work students should use.
- **Participation routine:** Roles, turn-taking, accountability, and equitable-access expectations.
- **Teacher monitoring guidance:** What to listen for and how observations inform facilitation.
- **Possible follow-up questions:** Flexible questions that clarify, extend, challenge, or connect ideas.
- **Transition:** How discussion findings return to whole-group learning or the next task.

Discussion guidance should enable responsive teaching rather than prescribe a rigid exchange.

## Vocabulary Design

The Instruction Designer prioritizes vocabulary essential for comprehension, participation, and the lesson objective. It must:

- preserve curriculum-provided words and definitions;
- create accurate student-friendly explanations when support is justified;
- connect words to the text, concept, or task;
- add contextual examples without inventing source claims;
- identify useful morphology or cognates when accurate;
- plan repeated exposure across the lesson;
- include opportunities for students to use important words;
- avoid overloading the lesson with too many terms.

Every added explanation, example, morphology connection, cognate, or practice opportunity must be labeled as a TeacherOS enhancement. Curriculum instruction and TeacherOS support must remain distinguishable.

## Reading Design

Reading design must:

- establish a clear purpose before students read;
- identify the intended reading mode;
- preserve required selections and source sequence;
- divide long or complex text into meaningful sections when justified;
- plan purposeful pauses for thinking, evidence gathering, clarification, or discussion;
- identify evidence students should locate or use;
- support comprehension without replacing the reading;
- preserve productive struggle and text-based reasoning;
- avoid unnecessary reproduction of copyrighted text.

Chunking, summaries, visuals, questions, and vocabulary support should provide access to the required reading, not become substitutes for it.

## ELD and Accessibility Design

ELD and accessibility design connects support to the actual content, language, sensory, procedural, or participation demands of the lesson. It may include:

- language objectives;
- language functions;
- language forms;
- sentence frames;
- visual supports;
- modeling;
- chunking;
- structured partner interaction;
- word banks;
- multiple ways to participate or demonstrate understanding;
- accommodations already present in the curriculum;
- optional extensions for advanced learners.

The design must distinguish universal supports, targeted multilingual learner supports, curriculum-provided accommodations, and extensions. Supports should increase access without lowering the central learning goal or doing the essential thinking for students.

## Checks for Understanding

Checks for understanding should appear at decision points such as:

- before instruction, to reveal relevant readiness;
- during modeling, to confirm attention to the demonstrated process;
- after guided practice, to evaluate readiness for reduced support;
- before independent work, to identify unresolved confusion;
- at lesson closure, to assess progress toward the central goal.

Possible checks include quick responses, partner explanations, annotations, brief written responses, evidence selection, oral rehearsal, and confidence checks. Confidence reports may supplement but must not replace evidence of understanding.

Every check must state its purpose, expected evidence, aligned objective, participation method, and likely teacher response. Added checks must be labeled as TeacherOS enhancements.

## Misconception Response Planning

For each supported misconception, the design records:

- **Likely error:** The misunderstanding or incorrect response pattern.
- **Evidence:** The source guidance or analysis supporting the concern.
- **Prevention strategy:** A concise instructional move intended to reduce the likelihood of the error.
- **Teacher prompt:** A question or cue that helps surface or address the misconception.
- **Corrective explanation:** Accurate, concise guidance that preserves student thinking.
- **Follow-up check:** Evidence that would show whether understanding improved.

Tentative misconceptions must remain labeled tentative. The design should not overbuild instruction around unsupported predictions.

## Assessment Design

The Instruction Designer preserves all required assessments and connects them to objectives and success criteria. It may clarify directions, define expected evidence, and provide answer guidance when appropriate and source-grounded or transparently added.

Added formative checks must align with the central or supporting objectives and provide actionable evidence. The module should avoid busywork, redundant measurement, and checks that assess unprepared content.

When the curriculum lacks sufficient evidence of learning, the module may propose an addition. The proposal must be labeled as a TeacherOS enhancement, include a rationale, and remain distinct from required curriculum assessment.

## Teacher Language

Teacher language should be concise, natural, classroom-ready, age appropriate, academically accurate, and easy to scan in speaker notes. It may include directions, questions, explanations, brief think-alouds, transitions, and responsive follow-ups.

Guidance should be adaptable rather than overly scripted. Exact wording is appropriate when precision matters, but the design should not force the teacher to read a continuous script word for word.

## Transitions

Transitions should:

- explain why the class is moving to the next activity;
- connect the thinking or evidence from one phase to the next;
- minimize procedural confusion;
- preserve instructional momentum;
- clarify changes in materials, location, timing, or grouping.

Transitions should be brief and functional. They must not add unrelated content or obscure changes made to the source sequence.

## Materials and Preparation

The materials and preparation plan defines:

- all required materials;
- materials originally required by the curriculum;
- TeacherOS-added materials;
- advance preparation;
- printing and copying needs;
- technology and access requirements;
- optional substitutions;
- missing-material alerts.

Every added or substituted material must identify its purpose and adaptation status. A substitution must preserve the task’s learning and access demands or be escalated for teacher approval.

## Differentiation

The Instruction Designer may include:

- access supports;
- strategic grouping;
- optional challenge;
- alternative response modes;
- additional modeling;
- reduced linguistic load without reduced intellectual demand;
- extension tasks.

Differentiation must remain connected to the same central objective unless the teacher explicitly authorizes an alternate goal. Supports should address identified demands, not assumptions about fixed student ability. Extensions should deepen or broaden meaningful thinking rather than add volume alone.

## Teacher Experience

The completed lesson design should be immediately understandable, low preparation, clearly sequenced, realistic, flexible, and easy to teach from slides and notes. Required, supporting, optional, and adapted elements must be easy to distinguish.

Teachers should be able to locate the lesson purpose, materials, actions, questions, expected evidence, supports, pacing, transitions, and unresolved decisions without reconstructing the design across disconnected records.

## Student Experience

Students should experience a lesson that is coherent, interactive, appropriately challenging, supportive, purposeful, predictable without being repetitive, and centered on meaningful thinking.

Students should understand what they are learning, why each task matters, what participation requires, and how success will be demonstrated. The design should provide preparation and support while preserving opportunities for productive struggle and independent performance.

## Curriculum Fidelity Record

Every major lesson-design decision must preserve:

- **Original curriculum requirement:** The source content, task, sequence, timing, assessment, or expectation affected.
- **Source file:** The registered source containing the requirement.
- **Page number:** The printed or digital page reference when available.
- **Section heading:** The nearest stable source locator.
- **TeacherOS decision:** The preserved, clarified, enhanced, requested, or proposed design action.
- **Adaptation type:** The applicable adaptation-status label.
- **Rationale:** The learning, access, coherence, pacing, or feasibility basis for the decision.
- **Effect on pacing:** No effect, time added, time redistributed, multi-day implication, or other explicit impact.
- **Effect on rigor:** Confirmation that demand is preserved, or a transparent description of any authorized change.
- **Fidelity status:** The decision’s relationship to the curriculum and its approval state.
- **Confidence level:** The strength of source and analytical support for the decision.

Fidelity statuses are:

- **Preserved:** The requirement remains substantively unchanged.
- **Clarified:** Presentation or directions are clearer without changing the task or demand.
- **Enhanced:** TeacherOS adds aligned support, interaction, modeling, or evidence while retaining the original goal.
- **Teacher-authorized change:** The teacher has explicitly approved a substantive change.
- **Required deviation:** A deviation is necessary for delivery under confirmed constraints and requires visible handling.
- **Unresolved:** Evidence, authority, or feasibility is insufficient for a final decision.

The record must keep curriculum source, TeacherOS design, teacher authority, and unresolved status distinguishable.

## Internal Workflow

The Instruction Designer follows this observable workflow:

1. Validate the Curriculum Analyzer output.
2. Confirm the central learning goal and evidence of mastery.
3. Preserve and map all required lesson components.
4. Select an appropriate lesson architecture.
5. Design the instructional sequence.
6. Add modeling, participation, discussion, vocabulary, ELD, and accessibility supports where justified.
7. Add checks for understanding.
8. Address pacing, barriers, and misconceptions.
9. Design transitions, teacher guidance, and student tasks.
10. Document every adaptation and preserve source traceability.
11. Populate the instructional-design sections of the Structured Lesson Model.
12. Pass the completed lesson design to the Slide Designer.

Each stage produces observable records, decisions, or validation statuses. This workflow does not expose or depend upon private reasoning.

## Output Contract

The Instruction Designer outputs:

- **Complete instructional sequence:** The ordered, classroom-ready progression of lesson phases.
- **Lesson-phase map:** Purpose, objective, timing, support, assessment, source, and adaptation relationships.
- **Teacher actions:** Clear facilitation, modeling, questioning, monitoring, and response guidance.
- **Student actions:** Clear thinking, participation, reading, writing, discussion, and production expectations.
- **Teacher-language guidance:** Concise, adaptable classroom language.
- **Discussion plan:** Purpose, structure, preparation, evidence, participation, monitoring, and follow-up.
- **Vocabulary-instruction plan:** Priorities, explanations, context, reuse, and source-versus-enhancement status.
- **Reading-instruction plan:** Purpose, mode, chunking, pauses, evidence, and comprehension support.
- **ELD and accessibility supports:** Demand-aligned language and access supports.
- **Checks for understanding:** Timed, purposeful evidence and teacher decision points.
- **Misconception-response plan:** Supported concerns, prevention, response, and follow-up.
- **Assessment plan:** Required assessments, added formative evidence, alignment, and guidance.
- **Pacing plan:** Source timing, proposed timing, transitions, load, and multi-day implications.
- **Transition plan:** Conceptual and procedural connections across phases.
- **Materials and preparation plan:** Required, added, substituted, missing, printing, and technology needs.
- **Differentiation plan:** Access, grouping, response, modeling, challenge, and extension decisions.
- **Curriculum adaptation log:** Every clarification, enhancement, teacher-requested change, and deviation.
- **Fidelity and traceability record:** Source, decision, rationale, effect, status, and confidence.
- **Unresolved-issues list:** Decisions or information still requiring review.
- **Enriched Structured Lesson Model:** The complete instructional design prepared for the Slide Designer.

The output must be structured, consistent, machine-readable, human-auditable, and classroom-ready. Curriculum-original content and TeacherOS design must remain visibly distinct.

## Validation Rules

Before handoff, the Instruction Designer verifies that:

- every lesson phase supports the central learning goal;
- all required curriculum components are preserved or explicitly flagged;
- all adaptations are labeled and justified;
- objectives and assessments remain aligned;
- student participation is intentional and academically meaningful;
- checks for understanding are included at useful decision points;
- teacher and student actions are clear;
- pacing is plausible and changes are visible;
- ELD and accessibility supports are present where identified needs justify them;
- academic rigor is preserved;
- source traceability remains intact;
- unresolved issues remain visible;
- required and optional elements are distinguishable;
- no final slide layout or rendering has been performed.

Validation findings must identify the affected phase or record, severity, rationale, and required action. Passing validation does not replace teacher approval.

## Failure Conditions

Design is blocked when:

- the central learning goal remains unresolved;
- required curriculum components are unavailable;
- essential source conflicts remain unresolved;
- the lesson cannot fit confirmed constraints without unauthorized omissions;
- a required assessment cannot be supported responsibly;
- the activity sequence is unsafe or unknown;
- design would require inventing major curriculum content.

Failure must produce a blocked-design report containing the blocking conditions, affected lesson components, safe partial design, consequences of proceeding, and recommended human decisions. The module must not conceal an omission or fabricate content to create the appearance of completion.

## Success Criteria

The Instruction Designer succeeds when:

- the lesson is coherent and teachable;
- all required curriculum content is preserved or transparently escalated;
- instructional decisions clearly support the central objective;
- students have meaningful opportunities to think and participate;
- modeling and support prepare students for increasing independence;
- supports increase access without lowering rigor;
- pacing and preparation are realistic;
- all enhancements and changes are transparent and traceable;
- unresolved issues remain visible;
- the Slide Designer receives a complete, classroom-ready instructional design.

## Version 1 Scope

Version 1 prioritizes:

- CKLA Grade 8;
- one lesson at a time;
- middle-school classroom instruction;
- teacher-guide fidelity;
- optional student-reader and activity-book materials;
- ELD supports;
- human review of unresolved issues;
- downstream lesson rendering through a presentation renderer.

Version 1 should establish a dependable instructional sequence, transparent adaptation process, practical teacher experience, meaningful student participation, and complete Slide Designer handoff before broader personalization is introduced.
